## Reading guide

Each agent below is a concrete, buildable unit of agentic-AI capability,
sized and scoped against a 2026 industry reality. They are not "AI
solutions" in the abstract; each one solves a specific revenue-relevant
problem and is measurable from week one. We list them in the order we
would build them — value-weighted and risk-weighted.

The proposal is structured around the CFO's gate: **Days 1–30**
diagnostic, **Days 31–60** two agents live, **Days 61–90** decide on the
remaining three. Capital outlay is in the low six figures, not the seven.
Payback in the conservative reading is nine to twelve months.

## Agent 1 — The USP <382> Wave-Capture Agent

### What it does
Continuously monitors every FDA Form 483 and warning letter for
elastomer functional-suitability gaps and container-closure compatibility
findings. Cross-references against CS Analytical's existing sponsor list
and target prospects. Auto-generates outreach drafts with the specific
exposure flag, citing the relevant FDA observation, and routes to BD.

### Why it's a 2026 agent (not a 2025 one)
USP <382> went official December 1, 2025. The 2026 warning-letter docket
already shows the pattern — Par Health USA (April 2026), Apollo Care
(February 2026) — with elastomer/closure findings as the leading
observation category. The demand pulse is dated and observable.

### Concrete sizing
50+ named sponsor programs with material exposure in the next 12 months.
At a 10% conversion-to-engagement rate, that is 5 named studies at typical
CCIT pricing, well above the agent's annualized cost in the first year.

### What we are *not* claiming
The agent does not draft scientific methods. It does not interact with
FDA. Every exposure flag passes through a human BD reviewer before any
outreach is sent.

### Data inputs
FDA warning-letter docket · FDA Form 483 database (via FOIA and
secondary aggregators) · CS Analytical's CRM and sponsor list · public
filings of named sponsors · USP <382> revision notices.

## Agent 2 — The Container-Format Radar

### What it does
Continuous ingestion of new container-format launches from SCHOTT,
Stevanato, BD, West, Bonfiglioli, and Gasporox. For each new format,
the agent identifies which sponsors are most likely to adopt (based on
pipeline composition, fill-finish CDMO relationships, and disclosed
container preferences) and estimates the method-development engagement
value.

### Why it's a 2026 agent
The 2025–2026 launches that defined this opportunity did not exist in
prior years:
- SCHOTT TOPPAC freeze (-180°C cell/gene), May 2025
- SCHOTT deep-cold prefilled syringe (-100°C drugs), May 2025
- Stevanato Nexa Flex (COP/COC), January 2025
- SCHOTT TOPPAC infuse (RFID + tamper-evident), January 2025
- Bonfiglioli IVB Flex (IV-bag vacuum decay), April 2026

Each is a discrete CCIT method-development engagement waiting to be
scoped. None of the broad-line generalists has validated methods for
the cryogenic-class formats.

### Concrete sizing
We estimate 8–15 commercial method-development opportunities per year
surfaced by this agent, weighted heavily toward the cell-and-gene
cryogenic segment which carries the highest unit margin in the CCIT
catalog.

### What we are *not* claiming
The agent does not design methods. It identifies opportunity. CS
Analytical's PhD bench (CSO-led) designs the method.

## Agent 3 — The Inspection-Readiness Mirror

### What it does
The FDA deployed agentic AI to its inspectors on December 1, 2025. CS
Analytical's clients need agents that *predict* what an AI-augmented
inspector will find. We build the mirror to FDA's tool — a continuously
updated "what would the inspector ask" engine that surfaces gaps in
client documentation, methods, and procedures against current FDA
enforcement patterns.

### Why this agent is uniquely defensible
The FDA's own tool is opaque to industry. CS Analytical's clients cannot
copy the FDA's agent — but they can buy a mirror from a specialist who
has invested in modeling FDA inspection behavior. This is the
highest-margin agent in the stack because the alternative (failing an
inspection) is so much more expensive than the service.

### Concrete sizing
Pricing as a premium retainer service to top 20 CS Analytical sponsor
relationships. Six-figure annual recurring revenue per relationship is
achievable at the upper end; mid-five-figure at the conservative end.

### What we are *not* claiming
The agent does not replace inspection-readiness consulting work. It
augments the consultant by surfacing the patterns no human reviewer can
read at scale. Every output is annotated as suggestion, not directive.

## Agent 4 — The CAR-T / Cell-Therapy Cryo Method Pipeline

### What it does
Targeted radar on every CAR-T and cell-therapy program in Phase II/III.
Maps their container choices (cryo-bag vs. vial vs. closed-system),
identifies which programs are nearing pivotal/commercial filings, and
quantifies the method-development opportunity.

### Why CS Analytical specifically
Manufacturing failure rates in CAR-T programs are non-trivial:
3.87% in large B-cell lymphoma (UK National CAR-T Panel data, 2025);
up to 25% in non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Cryopreservation quality is
identified as a critical determinant of overall manufacturing success.
Each prevented manufacturing failure preserves the patient's treatment
window and avoids the substantial cost (~$400–600K per CAR-T course)
of a failed manufacture.

CS Analytical's CSO Brandon Zurawlow has publicly presented on
cryogenic CCIT — including an Xtalks webinar specifically on container
closure integrity testing for ultracold storage and transport. There
is no other credentialed CCIT specialty laboratory in the U.S. that
can claim this expertise on the public record.

### Concrete sizing
8–12 active CAR-T programs in Phase II/III at any given moment;
3–5 of them in container/closure decision windows where a credentialed
specialty CCIT laboratory is the natural counterparty.

## Agent 5 — The Strategic-Optionality Tracker (CFO-only)

### What it does
A private, board-friendly view that continuously monitors comparable-
transaction multiples, strategic-acquirer activity (Nelson Labs explicitly
exploring acquisitions per Sotera Health Q1 2026 call, Eurofins
still building, West vertically integrating), competitor strategic
moves, and the CS Analytical revenue and pipeline position. Quarterly
delivered to the CFO as a single PDF.

### Why this agent matters
It is the first agent in the stack that pays for the full stack on its
own. A CFO who can walk into a board meeting with "this is what the
comparable transactions have done in the last 90 days, here is where
we sit, here is the strategic optionality" — without spending a week
assembling the slide deck — has bought time at a margin no other
deliverable can match.

The Gibraltar precedent (Nelson Labs' August 2018 acquisition of
Gibraltar Laboratories, a Fairfield NJ specialty analytical lab) makes
this agent particularly timely. The structural parallel to CS
Analytical is close enough that a serious M&A conversation could
happen on relatively short notice.

### Why CFO-only
The intelligence is sensitive. Sharing it widely changes the
information environment inside the company. The output is sized for the
CFO chair and the chair only — with selective sharing under CFO
discretion.

### Concrete sizing
Single-PDF quarterly deliverable. The agent runs continuously; the
human-curated output is bounded so the CFO actually reads it.

## How the five agents compound

The five agents are not five separate tools. They share a sponsor
entity model, a regulatory-events corpus, and a market-radar feed.
Building one reduces the marginal cost of the next. The full stack
operating together is materially more valuable than the sum of the
parts: Agent 1 (wave-capture) feeds Agent 4 (CAR-T pipeline) when an
elastomer finding hits a cell-therapy program; Agent 2 (container
radar) feeds Agent 3 (inspection mirror) when a new container format
creates inspection-readiness gaps in existing sponsor filings; Agent 5
(optionality) consumes all four to keep the CFO chair informed.

This is the case for building them as one platform, not five point
solutions.

## The 90-day engagement, restated

**Days 1–30 — Diagnostic.** No agents deployed. Three written
artifacts delivered to the CFO:
1. An exposure map — top 20 named sponsors with USP <382> or Annex 1
   method-update exposure in the next 12 months.
2. A market-radar pipeline seed — named prospects with evidence trail.
3. A regulatory-intelligence trial output — one week of USP / EP / FDA
   monitoring against the CS Analytical current method portfolio.

Each artifact is independently useful even if the engagement does not
progress past Day 30.

**Days 31–60 — Two agents live.** Wave-capture (Agent 1) and container-
format radar (Agent 2) wired to real data feeds. Daily output to a
named internal user. First measurable lift inside the period.

**Days 61–90 — Decide.** Joint readout. Numbers. Agents 3, 4, and 5
built only if Agents 1 and 2 have earned them. Fixed pricing at
engagement start; the only variable is the company's decision to
proceed. The CFO holds the gate.

## Commercial structure

Two viable structures. We are agnostic between them and would expect
the CFO chair to drive the choice.

**Structure A — Fixed engagement fee + annual platform license.** Clean.
Predictable. Easy to budget. Best when the buyer wants to keep the
relationship transactional.

**Structure B — Smaller fixed fee + revenue share on the productized
CCIT-as-a-service tier the agents enable.** Aligns incentives. Lower
upfront cost. Best when the buyer wants TSI-Citadel to have skin in
the long-term outcome.

Either structure is built around clear, audit-friendly definitions of
revenue attribution.
